Energy Bridge Critical Infrastructure Hackathon · Vienna, 23–25 Oct 2026 · Grid-Serving Storage track

Gridzilla: a grid signaling platform for grid-serving storage

A DSO-controlled platform that sends signed charge and discharge signals to home batteries in a congested grid segment, reaching Victron, GoodWe and any other system through three vendor-agnostic paths, with the owner's limits and a fail-safe enforced at the device.

The problem

Home batteries are now the largest storage fleet in Europe, and the grid operator cannot see or use a single one of them. In a crisis everyone gets cut equally, including the houses that could have ridden through.

Slovakia nothing at DSO level

No Slovak distribution operator sends signals to home storage. The only flexibility pilot, SEPS and Nano Energies with Stredoslovenská distribučná in 2022 to 2023, served the transmission operator's ancillary services, not the street transformer.

Germany the best case is a dimmer switch

§14a EnWG, in force since 2024, lets a DSO dim new batteries, wallboxes and heat pumps to 4.2 kW. Grid charging only, never discharge, never pre-charge, through certified gateways. Used in under one percent of hours.

Ukraine three gigawatt hours, zero coordination

Almost 3 GWh of batteries installed in 2025, five times 2024, mostly in homes. Ukrenergo publishes outage queues over Telegram and people charge by hand before their slot. Winter 2025 to 2026 brought blackouts over 48 hours in sub-zero weather.

Gridzilla the missing signal

One signed, bounded, vendor-agnostic signal from the DSO to that fleet. Congestion is the everyday justification. Outage preparation is the reason it matters.

What it is worth, honestly

  • Per street, little; per penetration, a lot. Four houses with 70 kWh barely move a 250 kVA transformer. A sixty-house street with fifteen batteries at 5 kW is 75 kW, about 30 percent of the transformer, for two to three hours. That is the difference between shedding the street and not.
  • In a crisis the unit is hours, not kilowatts. Houses in the program ride through their shedding queue on a battery the platform filled the night before. That is measurable, and it is what an operator under attack would pay for today.
  • For the DSO the money is in deferral. Fewer transformer upgrades, less curtailment. Germany wrote §14a for exactly that reason, which is the proof that the economic case exists.
  • Vehicles come next. An EV is a 60 kWh battery with wheels. Same signals, one new registry field: which segment the car is plugged into right now. Scaling story, not part of the deliverable.

Pitch in thirty seconds

A street transformer is hit, or the country is short of power, and the operator starts cutting streets. On that street sit seventy kilowatt hours of batteries that nobody told to charge last night. Gridzilla is the signal that fills them before the event, drains them while the grid is short, and rations them while the street is dark. It works on Victron, GoodWe and anything else, it never gives the operator raw control, and every house keeps it on a leash.

1. The big picture

The DSO never talks to an inverter directly. It publishes a signal for a grid segment. The signal travels over one of three paths and ends in a local policy agent next to the battery. That agent is the only thing that writes to the inverter, and it always merges the signal with the owner's own limits.

Gridzilla is the DSO's monster. It stomps on congestion, but every house keeps it on a leash.

Why the platform is the main point. Vendor clouds, local agents and standards gateways all change over time. The platform's job is to own the one thing that must stay stable: a signed, bounded, segment-scoped signal with a clear meaning. Vendors integrate with that signal once, on whichever path suits them.

2. The signal

A signal is an envelope at the grid connection point, never a device setpoint. The DSO says how much the home may import or export and for how long. The home decides how to achieve it with whatever hardware it has.

ABSORB PV surplus congestion

Midday, feeder voltage rising. Signal: lower the export limit and allow grid charging. The battery soaks up local PV instead of pushing it onto the feeder.

SUPPORT load peak congestion

Evening, heat pumps and chargers on. Signal: cap the import, "take 3 kW only", and when the street needs more, require a minimum feed-in, "give at least 2 kW". The battery covers the house and, if asked, the neighbours.

RELEASE event over

Explicit end of the event. Also implied when the window expires or the heartbeat stops. Owner mode resumes.

3. Resilience: the outage scenario

Congestion is the everyday case. The case that makes Gridzilla matter is the one from the flipchart: a transformer is destroyed, as in Ukraine, or a region runs short of generation and the DSO has to shed load. The platform prepares the street before the event and stretches the batteries through it.

Segments are a tree, not a transformer

Two villages on the same 22 kV feeder, say Atzgersdorf and Mauer in Liesing, each have their own street transformer. If Atzgersdorf's batteries feed in, the power goes up through Atzgersdorf's transformer in reverse, along the 22 kV line, and down into Mauer. That relieves the feeder and the 110/22 kV substation, which on the edge of Vienna are the usual bottleneck. It does not relieve Mauer's own transformer, and Atzgersdorf's transformer becomes the new limit in reverse.

What the flipchart got right and what to change

  • Right: slow, coordinated charging before the event. Synchronized charging is itself a transformer overload and worsens an upstream deficit, so PREPARE carries a deadline and a power cap and the agent schedules the charge into the cheapest, quietest hours.
  • Right: transformer data to the DSO. Load and status of the transformer is the trigger for every regime and the proof for settlement.
  • Right: program membership. The OK and BL houses on the sketch are the registry: signals only ever reach registered devices behind that transformer.
  • Change: "discharge when energy is valuable" means discharge while the grid is up but short. That is self-supply, not export. A destroyed transformer cannot be fed from the street.
  • Change: the control plane will be cut in exactly the moments it matters. Home routers die with the street. So PREPARE and STRETCH are signed schedules with a validity window that the agent executes offline, and a GX LTE modem or similar is the recommended out-of-band path. Live heartbeats stay for the congestion limits only, where losing contact must revert to owner mode.
  • Add: STRETCH and RECOVER. Stretching the battery over a known restoration time and recharging the street gently afterwards are the two signals the sketch was missing.
  • Security consequence: an attacker who controls the platform could drain every battery the night before a strike. The policy guard therefore treats PREPARE and STRETCH as owner-favourable only: they can raise target SoC and lower discharge, never the reverse. SUPPORT is the only signal that spends energy, and it is capped in depth and duration as before.

4. Enforcement and settlement

A DSO will not pay for a promise, and a household can switch any agent off. Gridzilla does not solve that with control. It solves it by measuring at a point the household cannot touch and paying only for what the meter proves.

Limits are verifiable, reductions are not

Demand-response schemes that pay for a reduction against a baseline get gamed by inflating the baseline. Every Gridzilla signal is a limit at the connection point: a ceiling on import, a ceiling on export, or a floor on export. Each is a yes or no question per 15-minute interval on the DSO's own smart meter. There is no counterfactual to argue about.

StepMechanismWhat it stops
1 · Meter is the judgeSettlement runs on the DSO smart meter at the connection point, never on inverter telemetry. Agent acks give real-time awareness and audit onlyFaked telemetry
2 · No ack, no payThe agent signs acknowledgements with a device key. Switched off or tampered with, the signatures stopSilent opt-out while collecting the fee
3 · Compliance gates the perksAvailability fee and the relaxed feed-in limit are paid against measured compliance per quarter. Three strikes a year and the household leaves the program and loses the connection terms that got it inJoining for the perk and ignoring the signals
4 · The DSO's existing hammerSmart meters already have remote disconnect and load limiting. Program members are exempt from shedding only while the meter shows compliance. Cheat during a peak and you are cut with everyone elseCheating in the one moment it matters

Why the DSO prefers this to control

A DSO that takes raw control of a customer's inverter takes on the battery, the warranty, the fire and the complaint. Pay-on-meter keeps the DSO where it already is: it owns the meter, sets the limit, measures compliance and settles. The household owns the device and the risk. That is the split the German regulator chose, and the one a Slovak DSO could adopt without new hardware.

What the household gets

  • Connection and feed-in rights. Join the program and get connected, or get a higher feed-in limit, because the DSO can now bound your behaviour in the rare hours it matters. Worth more than any payment where transformers are full.
  • Availability fee per kilowatt of declared flexibility per year, paid against compliance. Germany pays 110 to 190 € a year per device, or a 60 percent grid-fee discount.
  • Payment per kWh delivered under forced feed-in, measured at the meter.
  • In a crisis: a full battery before the outage, a shorter shedding queue, priority restoration, and rationing that turns an eight-hour battery into a twenty-hour one.
  • Protection: owner-set minimum SoC the DSO can never touch, a cap on cycles per year, opt-out from the app, aggregate data only.

5. Inside the local policy agent

This is where cybersecurity and owner protection live, and it is the same logic on all three paths. For the demo it is a Node-RED flow on a Cerbo GX. In a vendor cloud it would be the same rules in firmware.

7. As built: the Victron to DSO control plane

This is the stack that ran on the evening of 23 October, with a real Cerbo GX at the test site acknowledging a signed signal from the DSO platform. Nothing in it needs port forwarding, a vendor cloud, or a change to the inverter.

One signal through the stack

StepWhereWhat happens
1BoardOperator composes SUPPORT for tf:atzgersdorf-1, or loads the advisor's proposal. Guard checks mode, fields, window, rate, reverse rating.
2PlatformSequence number incremented, document serialised, signed with the platform's Ed25519 key, published to gridzilla/sig/tf:atzgersdorf-1, QoS 1.
3GX, mqtt inThe flow is subscribed to every node on its path, so it receives signals for its transformer, its feeder, its substation and the region.
4GX, verifySignature over the envelope bytes with the pinned platform key. Then seq greater than last, issued within 120 s, window not expired, segment on my path, cohort match.
5GX, mergeOwner limits win: minimum SoC from ESS, charge and discharge caps, feed-in limit. Partial or refused is a valid outcome with a reason.
6GX, applyCurrent ESS values saved once. After a random ramp delay, the Victron output nodes write the limits to com.victronenergy.settings.
7GX, ackAck document with status, applied limits, SoC and grid kW, signed with the device key, published to gridzilla/ack/vic-atzgersdorf-07.
8PlatformAck signature verified against the registered device key, logged, shown on the board and counted in the node aggregates.
9GX, watchdogEvery 5 s: at window end, or after 120 s without a valid heartbeat for live modes, the saved ESS values are written back and a reverted ack is sent.

10. What goes into the IES profile, and what stays in the pitch

  • Profile (the deliverable): actors, the congestion signals and the four resilience signals, the transactions (register, publish signal, acknowledge, heartbeat, release, report), the security requirements per transaction, and the owner-protection rules. No protocols, no registers.
  • Pitch (evidence): the Victron mapping, the GoodWe mapping and the live demo, to show the profile is implementable on hardware sold today.
  • Open questions for the mentors: who runs the segment registry, how registration is tied to the meter point, and whether Path C must be mandatory for compensation.
